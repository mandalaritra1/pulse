    Tips for a clean reading
    • Rest your fingertip lightly over both the lens and the flash. Pressing hard squeezes the blood out and flattens the signal.
    • Keep still. For 30 s and HRV, rest the phone on a table and sit comfortably.
    • Cold fingers give weak signals: warm them up first.
    • For HRV, measure at the same time each day (e.g. after waking), breathe normally, and don't talk.
    • If you lift your finger during HRV, recording pauses; put it back and it continues. Intervals across the gap are never counted.
    • Estimates only, not a medical device.
    What the HRV numbers mean
    RMSSD
    Root-mean-square of successive beat-to-beat differences. Tracks fast (parasympathetic, "rest and digest") variation. The standard daily-tracking metric; compare against your own baseline, not other people.
    SDNN
    Standard deviation of all clean beat intervals: total variability over the recording.
    pNN50
    % of successive intervals differing by more than 50 ms.
    SD1 / SD2
    Width / length of the Poincaré cloud: short-term vs long-term variability.
    Stress index
    Baevsky's index from the interval histogram. Higher means a narrower, more rigid rhythm.
    LF, HF, LF/HF
    Power of interval oscillations at 0.04–0.15 Hz and 0.15–0.4 Hz (Lomb–Scargle). HF mostly follows breathing. Needs ≥ 2 min; physiological interpretation of LF/HF is debated.
    Breathing
    Your heart speeds up on each in-breath and slows on each out-breath; the dominant period of that rhythm gives breaths per minute.
    Timing precision
    Estimated uncertainty of each beat's timing. Its contribution is subtracted from RMSSD and SDNN, so camera noise doesn't inflate them.
